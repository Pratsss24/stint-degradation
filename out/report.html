The whole problem in one line. Within a stint, tyre age and race lap move together one for one, so leaving the race lap out does not drop the fuel effect — it hides it inside the degradation coefficient. The naive estimate below is therefore not a noisier version of the right answer; it is the right answer plus the fuel effect, which here is large enough to reverse the sign.

A drying track makes the whole field faster lap after lap, which is a race-lap effect no fuel load can produce, and it swamps the model. The measure is the spread of the field median over the race. It runs from 0.012 to 0.050 for 20 races and then jumps to 0.124; the cut at 0.08 sits in that empty band, so anywhere in it removes the same races.

With the condition rule on, the estimate moves by 0.0119 s per lap across every combination of the two constants. With it off, the safety-car threshold alone moves it by 0.0990 — more than the effect being measured. The fragile constant was never the safety-car rule; it was the two races the rule was being asked to paper over.

Traffic, and why slow laps are not deleted

Being held up adds time to a lap and never subtracts it, so the residuals are skewed one way. The robust fit down-weights the far tail symmetrically; removing slow laps as a class would remove the end of every stint, which is where the degradation is.
